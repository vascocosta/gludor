First flight
  1. Launch. Take the winch or follow the tug; release with SPACE when the climb flattens.
  2. Pick a field. Home has the long asphalt runway. Nearby towns add short grass strips with winch or air starts; aerotow stays at home.
  3. Keep a strip in reach. The top-right field cue points to the nearest runway and shows its designator and length.
  4. Find lift. Cumulus mark the thermals. Turn tightly, keep the slip ball centred, watch the vario, and follow the top-right arrow until it turns green.
  5. Read the ridge. The long slope north of the field works when the wind blows onto it.
  6. Land. Airbrakes are strong. Arrive high, fly a normal circuit, and touch down wings level. Use Q/E to steer the roll-out and B for the wheel brake; the summary opens when you stop.

Soaring notes

  • Thermals widen with height. The strongest lift is usually in the core, not at the edge.
  • Use 40–50° of bank in a thermal and keep the slip ball centred. Slower is not always better.
  • Ridge lift is smooth and mechanical. Stay inside the band of lift along the windward slope.
  • Total-energy vario compensates for speed changes. Pull up and it will read the energy you are trading.
  • Airbrakes are your friend on approach. Full boards give a steep, controllable descent.
  • Below 20 m the HUD shows a flare strip. Watch the height marker enter the green band and let the sink settle.
